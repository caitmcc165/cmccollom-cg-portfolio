      05 / Independent game concept & guided prototype · In progress

      Arkfall Frontier

      What happens when competitive rules shape not just battles, but alliances, trust, cooperation, and the long-term health of a game world?

      Personal game systems conceptStrategy & player experience

      Arkfall Frontier is my in-development multiplayer space strategy and survival concept. My focus is designing how interconnected systems influence the choices players make: when to compete, when to cooperate, what to protect, and why coming back tomorrow should feel worthwhile.

      The design challenge

      Many persistent strategy games risk letting unequal spending power and entrenched alliances determine every outcome. I wanted progression that still rewards investment without making meaningful competition inaccessible or removing reasons to participate.

      Decisions I explored

      Rotating Facilities require uninterrupted control to claim; Forts use specified competition windows and voting; coalition structures create shared incentives. I also explored collectible cosmetic rewards and accumulation bonuses while keeping event participation meaningful for free and low-spending players.

      Why they matter

      These rules are intended to create contestable objectives, decisions under pressure, and social coordination—not simply add complexity. I consider how a rule could change alliance behavior, engagement, and the balance between short-term wins and long-term participation.

      A game-systems thought experiment

      What should a contested objective reward?

      Imagine an objective owned by the strongest alliance indefinitely. Adding rotating eligibility and a defined control window could change who participates, which alliances cooperate, and how leaders plan. But it could also increase coordination demands. The interesting work is debating those tradeoffs before deciding which version creates the better player experience.

      This is a design rationale, not evidence that the mechanics have been implemented or validated with real players. The concept also includes a guided Unity prototype testing limited resource, reactor, expedition, event, and save/load behavior; there is no shipped commercial product or established player-base data.
