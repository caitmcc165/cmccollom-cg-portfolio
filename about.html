My career has been shaped by recognizing opportunities for growth and turning them into practical improvements. I bring together business judgment, collaborative leadership, and technology to create better experiences for customers and the teams who serve them.

My professional approach

Seeing what could be. Making it work.

Throughout my career, I've been drawn to opportunities to make a business more effective, whether that means developing a new service, improving the customer experience, or connecting people and processes through technology. I look at how the pieces of a business work together, identify where a change can create meaningful value, and develop practical solutions that people can use and sustain.

At Technical Difference, I helped develop an organized software-training function by establishing consistent scheduling, expense controls, client coordination, and operating procedures. Working with trainers, Sales, Accounting, and leadership, I helped turn training into a profitable service and create a more reliable experience for clients.

At White Dragon Martial Arts, I saw an opportunity to expand access to our programs and strengthen the student experience. I established partnerships with seven charter schools, standardized enrollment and renewal practices, introduced a shared scheduling system, and helped develop our instructor team. During my tenure, sustained enrollment at the Temecula school grew from 86 to 172 students.

At TAG Chemical Technologies, I established the operating foundation of a newly independent business. I developed order fulfillment, supplier coordination, billing, and financial controls, supporting approximately 200 commercial customer accounts. I also led the evaluation and pilot of Supply Mover, working with its software engineers to translate business needs into system requirements and test how those workflows would function in practice.

These experiences have taught me that effective improvements require more than a good idea. They require an understanding of the business, consideration of the people affected, clear decisions about priorities, and collaboration with the people who will bring the solution to life. My B.S. in Business Management and M.S. in DevOps Software Engineering complement that experience with a foundation in business strategy and software delivery.

I bring this perspective to organizations looking to improve their operations, evolve their products, develop new capabilities, or strengthen customer experiences. My interest in software and games is a natural extension of that work: I enjoy exploring how systems, decisions, and human behavior interact to create experiences that people value.

Community leadership & product interests

Exploring strategy through games and communities.

Leading an alliance in Whiteout Survival, a multiplayer strategy game, has given me another setting in which to explore leadership, community engagement, and collaborative decision-making. I coordinate events, develop shared practices, mentor members, and work with fellow leaders to navigate competition and changing priorities.

That experience has deepened my interest in how game mechanics, incentives, and social systems influence player behavior. It also informs my approach to Nibble Fish and Arkfall Frontier, where I explore feature design, progression, player experience, and the interactions between game systems.

These interests connect closely with my professional strengths: understanding the broader system, considering different perspectives, and developing improvements that support a shared goal.

Project context: Alliance leadership is volunteer community experience. Nibble Fish and Arkfall Frontier are personal game projects in development, rather than released commercial titles.
