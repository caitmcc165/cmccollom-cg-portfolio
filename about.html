Hi, I'm Caitlin

I didn't exactly plan this career.

I never set out to have a career in business operations. I took the opportunities that came my way, got good at organizing things, and somehow kept ending up responsible for more of them. The funny part is that I never liked the administrative side much. What I liked was figuring out why something wasn't working, finding a better approach, and getting everyone moving in the same direction.

I love a good debate, especially with people who aren't afraid to tell me they think I'm wrong. I want to hear their reasoning, share mine, and work through the possibilities. I don't need to be the one who came up with the idea, or even the one who makes the final decision. I do want to know my opinion was considered. And if someone knows more than I do, I'm usually the first person asking them questions.

That's been the common thread in my work, even when the jobs looked nothing alike. At Technical Difference, I helped organize a software-training department. At White Dragon Martial Arts, I loved working with the instructors and students while helping the school grow. At TAG Chemical Technologies, I untangled order and billing processes and worked with a software vendor to figure out what a better system needed to do.

In 2026 I finished my M.S. in DevOps Software Engineering. That degree was different for me: I chose it because I was already curious about game development and wanted to understand the technology better. I genuinely like software, but I'm most interested in the decisions behind what we build—how it should work, what happens when we change something, and how people will respond. That's what I keep coming back to with my own projects, Nibble Fish and Arkfall Frontier.

I'd love to bring my experience with people, leadership, and complicated systems to a team working on games or software products. Not because I've already done every part of that job, but because those are the problems I actually want to spend my time thinking about.

One of my favorite places to lead

Yes, I take strategy games pretty seriously.

For almost two years, I've been leading an alliance in Whiteout Survival, a multiplayer strategy game. It might sound like an unusual place to talk about leadership, but keeping nearly 100 people engaged while navigating competition and state politics has been one of my favorite challenges.

I deliberately surround myself with leaders who will challenge me. I like figuring out why people make the choices they do, talking through disagreements, and giving capable people room to handle the things they're good at. I don't need everyone to agree with me, and I don't always get my way. I just want us to actually talk it through. And yes, sometimes the politics are exhausting. That's when I'm especially grateful for teammates I trust to step in.

It's also a big reason I love thinking about game design. Change one rule and you can change the way an entire group of people plays. I find that fascinating.

A quick distinction: My alliance leadership is volunteer community experience, not a paid game-industry job. Nibble Fish and Arkfall Frontier are still personal projects, not released commercial games.

I enjoy a challenge and even a tough disagreement. I don't enjoy drama for the sake of drama.
