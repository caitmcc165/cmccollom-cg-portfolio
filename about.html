Hi, I'm Caitlin

From understanding the system to shaping what comes next.

My career has taken me through software training, martial arts leadership, and business operations. But the part I love has never been the paperwork. It's understanding a complicated situation, recognizing what could work better, and helping people with different strengths move toward a shared result.

I'm energized by changing challenges, constructive debate, and the psychology behind people's decisions. I don't need to be the person who originated an idea—or the most senior person in the room. I want to work with thoughtful people whose expertise I respect, contribute meaningful judgment, and help shape the direction we take together.

At Technical Difference, I helped organize the operating side of a software-training function. At White Dragon Martial Arts, I co-led instructors, developed students, and improved programs and systems as the school's Assistant Chief Instructor. At TAG Chemical Technologies, I rebuilt order-to-delivery workflows and worked directly with a software vendor on requirements, testing, and a CRM pilot.

My M.S. in DevOps Software Engineering deepened my understanding of how software is built and delivered. Outside of formal work, I've been designing game systems for Nibble Fish and Arkfall Frontier, exploring how rules, player incentives, fairness, and collaboration shape the experience.

Today, I'm especially interested in contributing to game and software product decisions, player experiences, and strategic team coordination—bringing an established leadership and systems background into work I'm genuinely excited to pursue.

Leadership beyond a job title

I enjoy the strategy. And the people who make it work.

For nearly two years, I've helped lead a player alliance in the multiplayer strategy game Whiteout Survival. It's an unpaid community role, but it has taught me plenty about how incentives, competition, trust, and group dynamics shape what people do.

I enjoy navigating an evolving political landscape, debating ideas with other leaders, delegating execution to capable teammates, and coordinating events that keep people engaged. What matters most isn't winning an argument—it's being heard, listening seriously, and finding a decision the team can stand behind.

That same blend of people, strategy, and interconnected systems is what draws me to game design and product experiences.

Community experience, not paid game-industry employment. My alliance leadership reflects sustained volunteer coordination and game-system insight. My personal projects show design thinking and guided prototypes, not commercially shipped games.

I like meaningful friction: difficult decisions, competing priorities, and problems worth solving. I don't confuse that with a preference for toxic conflict or perpetual crisis management.
